# Literature findings

Checked 2026-10-26. This is a focused design review, not a systematic evidence review or a complete curriculum. Short original summaries are linked to their sources. No complete third-party lesson album is reproduced.

## Finding 1: curriculum areas are the right starting point

The proposed emphasis on Practical Life, Sensorial, Language, Mathematics, and other subjects is supported by the literature. AMI's overview describes these areas together with geography, history, biology, science, music, and the arts. It also emphasizes children's choice, pace, concrete experience, and guidance by a trained adult.

Source: [AMI: Montessori 3-6](https://montessori-ami.org/node/2175).

AMS groups early-childhood study into five areas, including Cultural Studies. Its cultural area includes history, geography, science, art, and music. Practical Life includes daily care and social interaction; Sensorial develops discrimination and classification; Language encompasses vocabulary, writing, and reading; Mathematics uses concrete experiences for number concepts.

Source: [AMS: Early Childhood](https://amshq.org/about-us/inside-the-montessori-classroom/early-childhood/).

Montessori Australia's curriculum lists four principal areas and integrates additional cultural and creative subjects. Its scope and sequence is expressed through materials and their typical presentations. Therefore, our taxonomy should support multiple groupings instead of declaring one universal count of subjects.

Source: [Montessori Australia: Three to Six](https://www.montessoricurriculum.org.au/learning-programme-from-3-to-6).

## Finding 2: the original literature also separates these domains

The Montessori Method contains chapters on Practical Life (VII), Education of the Senses (XII-XIV), reading and writing (XVI-XVII), language (XVIII), and numeration/arithmetic (XIX). These support the curriculum-area framing. The historical text includes dated language and practices; its historical status does not make every passage suitable for present-day instructional guidance.

Primary text: [The Montessori Method, online edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/39863/pg39863-images.html). [Edition and access information](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/39863).

## Finding 3: Spiral is a defensible product name

An AMS article explicitly discusses the spiral curriculum in Montessori: revisiting ideas at greater depth, connecting prior learning with later work, and linking subjects. It credits the term's formulation to Bruner rather than claiming Montessori coined it. Its geometry example follows materials from sensory exploration into more abstract concepts. The article also discusses limits to a single spiral model across disciplines.

Design inference: represent a connected curriculum with revisits and extensions. The product name does not justify a rigid linear progression, fixed age targets, or a single completion percentage.

Source: [AMS: Tracing the Spiral Curriculum](https://amshq.org/blog/montessori-education/2023-09-11-tracing-the-spiral-curriculum/).

## Finding 4: there are real progressions, but they need context

The mathematics curriculum describes concrete quantities, symbols, and their association, followed by repetition and increasing abstraction. It connects mathematics with prior practical-life and sensorial experience. This supports showing the purpose and connections of activities. It does not support automatically assigning a child's next lesson from one progress marker.

Source: [Montessori Australia: Mathematics, Three to Six](https://www.montessoricurriculum.org.au/learning-programme-from-3-to-6/mathematics).

## Finding 5: observations matter for personal records

AMI describes assessment through observation of work and abilities, with plans reviewed as observations develop. A dated record should distinguish what was observed, the observer's interpretation, and a proposed next action. A lack of a recorded mark should remain unknown, not become an assertion that a skill is absent.

Source: [AMI: Assessing Progress](https://montessori-ami.org/questions/assessing-student-progress).

## Finding 6: later levels need their own model

AMS describes elementary Practical Life extending into organization and community participation, familiar mathematical materials taking on new purposes, and integrated cultural studies. An early-childhood activity map cannot simply be relabeled as elementary material.

Source: [AMS: Elementary](https://amshq.org/about-us/inside-the-montessori-classroom/elementary/).

## What remains unverified

- Detailed activity-by-activity presentations and prerequisites need source-specific review by an experienced Montessori educator.
- The effectiveness of this app and its proposed adult side quests has not been studied.
- A note or correction from a contributor is a proposal, not automatically accepted educational guidance.
- Content should retain its source tradition, developmental context, review status, and revision history.
