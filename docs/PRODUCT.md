# Product scope

## Purpose

Make the Montessori curriculum understandable and usable for parents and educators, while helping adults build a thoughtful record of what they have learned, tried, questioned, and corrected.

## People

- A parent with limited Montessori knowledge needs plain explanations, a place to begin, and support for discussing an activity.
- An educator or experienced parent needs greater detail, source evidence, relationships between activities, and a way to propose corrections.
- Both need a learning ledger that preserves how their understanding changes over time.

These are different reading needs, not credentials assigned by the app. Completing side quests does not confer a teaching qualification.

## Curriculum structure

Start with the early-childhood curriculum as a bounded first reference set. Do not infer any individual child's age from this choice. The structure must accommodate later developmental levels separately.

Top-level areas:

1. Practical Life
2. Sensorial
3. Language
4. Mathematics
5. Cultural Studies, with Geography, History, Science, Art, and Music visible as browsable subareas.

Grace and courtesy and social development can cross areas. Different source traditions organize cultural subjects differently; retain provenance rather than claiming one universal taxonomy. See [literature findings](LITERATURE.md).

Use the hierarchy area -> strand -> activity/material -> supported concept. Connect related concepts across areas. A connection may mean preparation, related work, an extension, or revisiting an idea. Do not turn every connection into a mandatory prerequisite.

## Product loop

Explore an area -> understand an activity -> complete an adult side quest -> record a reflection -> revisit with deeper understanding -> add a correction when evidence changes.

The curriculum is the main structure. Observation, preparation, and respectful support are embedded in the relevant activity pages and quests.

## Scope boundaries

The first public release is an adult reference and learning tool. It does not grade children, predict readiness, prescribe an age deadline, replace an educator's observations, or claim to be an accredited teacher-training course.

The public edition contains generic educational content only. Child monitoring belongs to a separately secured private instance. A public UI should never advertise, link to, or preload a personalized instance.

Community-reviewed corrections are a later phase. Initial notes and proposed corrections remain local drafts until someone explicitly decides to share a sanitized contribution.
